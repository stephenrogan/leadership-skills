# Example: Manager One-on-One Prep

Skill: `manager-one-on-one-prep`
Data: synthetic

## Messy input

Priya has missed two project updates, but her customer calls are strong. She mentioned feeling stretched three weeks ago. Her manager wants to talk about accountability without making it punitive. There are also career-growth notes from last quarter that have not been revisited.

## Generic prompt baseline

> Ask Priya how she is doing, discuss the missed updates, and offer support. Balance accountability with empathy and revisit her development goals.

Problem: fine advice, but the manager still has to build the conversation.

## Skill-shaped output

# 1:1 Prep: Priya

## Manager objective

Understand whether the missed updates are a workload, clarity, motivation, or prioritization issue while preserving trust and setting a clear follow-up expectation.

## Evidence and signals

Facts:

- Two project updates missed.
- Customer calls remain strong.
- Priya previously said she felt stretched.
- Career-growth discussion from last quarter has not been revisited.

Interpretations:

- This may be a capacity or priority-management issue, not a performance drop.
- Strong customer calls suggest the issue is not broad disengagement.

Missing context:

- What work displaced the project updates
- Whether expectations were explicit
- Whether other deadlines are at risk

## Conversation flow

1. Start with context, not accusation.
2. Ask what got in the way of the two missed updates.
3. Separate workload reality from ownership expectations.
4. Agree the minimum update standard for the next month.
5. Reopen the career-growth thread and check whether current workload supports it.

## Questions

- What made the updates hard to complete?
- Was the expectation clear enough?
- What are you carrying that I may not see?
- What should we stop, sequence, or delegate?
- What career-growth work still feels important this quarter?

## Manager boundary

Do not label this as a formal performance issue from these notes alone. Prepare the conversation, gather evidence, and decide follow-up after hearing Priya's context.

## Follow-up

Owner: Manager  
Date: next 1:1 plus two-week check  
Success signal: updates land on time or risks are raised before the deadline

## Why this is better

The skill output gives the manager a usable conversation plan, separates fact from interpretation, and avoids overreaching from thin evidence.
